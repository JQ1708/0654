1. The Importance of Biodiversity

What is an Ecosystem?

(Tap to reveal)

An ecosystem is defined as a unit containing the community of organisms and their environment, interacting together.

e.g., a decomposing log, a lake

What is Biodiversity?

(Tap to reveal)

Defined as the number of different species that live in a particular area.

High biodiversity is needed for stable ecosystems.

Human Pressures

  • Human activities have tended to force biodiversity downwards.
  • Habitat Destruction by humans is a major downward pressure on biodiversity.

2. Habitat Destruction

The increasing human population of the planet is causing destruction of many habitats from rainforest to woodland to marine.

Many habitats are destroyed by humans to make space for other economic activities, or by pollution from these activities.

This reduces biodiversity and interrupts food chains and webs, meaning that more species may die because their prey is gone.

Main Reasons (Tap to Explore)

1
Clearing Land for Farming & Housing

Crops, livestock and homes all take up a large amount of space.

As there is an increasing population and demand for food, the amount of land available for these things must be increased by clearing habitats such as forests (Deforestation).

2
Extraction of Natural Resources

Natural resources such as wood, stone, and metals must be gathered to make different products.

  • Therefore many trees are cut down, destroying forest habitats.
  • Some resource extraction takes up a large amount of space (e.g., Mining), which means that the land must be cleared first.
3
Marine Pollution

Human activities lead to the pollution of marine habitats.

  • In many places, oil spills and other waste pollutes the oceans, killing sea life.
  • Eutrophication: Can occur when fertilizers from intensively farmed fields enter waterways.
  • Result: This causes a huge decrease in biodiversity in these areas as most aquatic species living in these waterways die from lack of oxygen.

3. Deforestation & Effects

Definition: The clearing of trees (usually on a large scale).

As the amount of Earth's surface covered by trees decreases, it causes increasingly negative effects on the environment and is a particularly severe example of habitat destruction.

Sustainable: If trees are replaced by replanting.
Not Sustainable: Generally cleared for land use:
  • Building
  • Grazing for cattle
  • Planting of monocultures (e.g., palm oil plantations)

Undesirable Effects (Extended Tier)

1. Extinction / Loss of Biodiversity

Forest habitats, especially tropical rainforests, have a huge range of biodiversity.

As habitat is destroyed it causes the loss of large numbers of plant and animal species. Many of these species are only found in these areas and therefore will become extinct.

2. Soil Erosion

  • Tree roots help to stabilise the soil, preventing it from being eroded by rain.
  • Trees usually take up nutrients and minerals from the soil through their roots.
  • Without trees, nutrients and minerals will remain unused in the soil so will be washed away into rivers and lakes by rain (Leaching).
  • This loss of soil nutrients is permanent and makes it very difficult for forest trees to regrow, even if the land is not cultivated with crop plants or grass for cattle.

3. Flooding

Without trees, the topsoil will be loose and unstable so will be easily washed away by rain.

Increases risk of flash flooding and landslides.

4. Increased CO₂ in Atmosphere

  • Trees carry out photosynthesis (take in CO₂, release O₂).
  • Removal of trees means less CO₂ is removed (and less O₂ released).
  • When cleared, trees are often burned (combustion) as opposed to being cut down. This releases CO₂.
  • Further increases CO₂ levels in atmosphere and contributes to Global Warming.

4. Endangered Species & Conservation

Endangered Species: At risk of becoming extinct.

Genetic Variation Risk:

A species may be at risk of becoming extinct if there is not enough genetic variation in the population.

Random changes in the environment may quickly cause extinction because the remaining organisms are all very similar and may not have the adaptations to survive such changes.

Why do they become endangered?

The population of the species may fall below a critical level due to:

Hunting Climate Change Pollution Loss of Habitat
Introduction of non-native species that outcompete native species

How to Conserve?

  • Education programmes
  • Captive breeding programmes
  • Monitoring and legal protection of the species and of their habitats
  • Seed Banks Seeds of endangered plant species are carefully stored so that new plants may be grown in the future

Why Conserve?

Reasons: Moral, Cultural, and Scientific.

Reducing extinction rates of both plant and animal species.
Keeping damage to food chains and food webs to a minimum and protecting vulnerable ecosystems (e.g., the rainforests).
Protecting our future food supply.
Maintaining nutrient cycles.
Possible sources of future medical drugs and fuels.
